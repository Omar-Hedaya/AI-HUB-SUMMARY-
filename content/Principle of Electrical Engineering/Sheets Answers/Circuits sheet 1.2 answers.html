📌 Key Laws, Formulas & Unit Conversions (Sheet 1A)
• Ohm's Law: $V = I \cdot R \implies I = \frac{V}{R} \implies R = \frac{V}{I}$
• Electric Power: $P = V \cdot I = I^2 \cdot R = \frac{V^2}{R} \quad (\text{in Watts, W})$
• Electrical Energy: $E = P \cdot t \quad (\text{Joule} = \text{W}\cdot\text{s} \text{ or } \text{kWh} = \text{kW}\cdot\text{h})$
• Appliance Efficiency: $\eta = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%$
• Wire Resistance: $R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$, where $A = \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}$
• Volume Invariance (Stretching): $\text{Vol} = A \cdot L = \text{const} \implies R \propto L^2 \propto \frac{1}{A^2} \propto \frac{1}{r^4}$
• 4-Band Color Code: $R = (D_1 D_2) \times 10^M \pm \text{Tolerance}\%$
• Unit Conversions: $1\text{ kW} = 10^3\text{ W}$, $1\text{ mm} = 10^{-3}\text{ m}$, $1\text{ hr} = 60\text{ min} = 3600\text{ s}$
Q1: Current in a Heating Element
Q1. A heating element has a constant electrical resistance of $22\ \Omega$. If it is connected across a standard $110\text{ V}$ voltage source, calculate the electric current passing through the element.
⚡ 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Resistance ($R$): $22\ \Omega$
• Supply Voltage ($V$): $110\text{ V}$
2. Circuit Diagram: Heating Element Connected to 110 V Source
3. Governing Law / Formula
By Ohm's Law, the electric current $I$ flowing through a resistor is directly proportional to the applied voltage $V$ and inversely proportional to its resistance $R$:
$$I = \frac{V}{R}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
$$I = \frac{110\text{ V}}{22\ \Omega} = 5\text{ A}$$
5. Final Result
$I = 5\text{ A}$
• Engineering Note: A standard $110\text{ V}$ circuit drawing $5\text{ A}$ dissipates $P = V \cdot I = 550\text{ W}$ of thermal heat.
Q2: Electric Filament Lamp Rating (220 V, 100 W)
Q2. An electric filament lamp is labeled with the rating specification ($220\text{ V}$, $100\text{ W}$). When operated normally at its rated voltage, calculate:
a) The electrical current passing through the lamp.
b) The resistance of the lamp's incandescent filament.
💡 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Rated Voltage ($V$): $220\text{ V}$
• Rated Power ($P$): $100\text{ W}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
• a) Electric Current from Power Rating: $$P = V \cdot I \implies I = \frac{P}{V}$$
• b) Filament Resistance: $$R = \frac{V^2}{P} = \frac{V}{I}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
• Part (a) Current passing through the lamp:
$$I = \frac{100\text{ W}}{220\text{ V}} = \frac{5}{11}\text{ A} \approx 0.4545\text{ A}$$
• Part (b) Resistance of the incandescent filament:
$$R = \frac{(220\text{ V})^2}{100\text{ W}} = \frac{48,400\text{ V}^2}{100\text{ W}} = 484\ \Omega$$
5. Final Result
a) $I = 0.4545\text{ A}\ (454.5\text{ mA})$  |  b) $R = 484\ \Omega$
• Engineering Note: $484\ \Omega$ is the hot operating resistance of the tungsten filament at incandescent temperature ($\sim 2500^\circ\text{C}$).
Q3: DC Motor Electrical Energy Consumption
Q3. An electric motor draws a current of $5\text{ A}$ from a $24\text{ V}$ DC power source. Determine the total electrical energy consumed by the motor during $30\text{ minutes}$ of continuous operation. Express your answer in Kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$).
⚙️ 1. Given Data (SI & Practical Units)

• Current ($I$): $5\text{ A}$
• Supply Voltage ($V$): $24\text{ V}$
• Operating Time ($t$): $30\text{ min} = \frac{30}{60}\text{ h} = 0.5\text{ hours} = 1800\text{ s}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
• Electrical Power: $$P = V \cdot I \quad (\text{W}) \implies P_{(\text{kW})} = \frac{V \cdot I}{1000}$$
• Electrical Energy: $$E_{(\text{kWh})} = P_{(\text{kW})} \times t_{(\text{hours})}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Calculate input power $P$:
$$P = 24\text{ V} \times 5\text{ A} = 120\text{ W} = \frac{120}{1000}\text{ kW} = 0.12\text{ kW}$$
2. Calculate total energy consumed in $t = 0.5\text{ h}$:
$$E = 0.12\text{ kW} \times 0.5\text{ h} = 0.06\text{ kWh}\quad (\text{or } 60\text{ Wh} = 216\text{ kJ})$$
5. Final Result
$E = 0.06\text{ kWh}\ (60\text{ Wh} = 216\text{ kJ})$
Q4: Electrical Appliance Efficiency
Q4. An electrical appliance consumes $1.2\text{ kW}$ of power when operating. If it converts $960\text{ W}$ into useful mechanical output power, calculate the efficiency of this appliance.
📊 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Input Electrical Power ($P_{\text{in}}$): $1.2\text{ kW} = 1.2 \times 10^3\text{ W} = 1200\text{ W}$
• Useful Mechanical Output Power ($P_{\text{out}}$): $960\text{ W}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$\eta (\%) = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\%$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
$$\eta = \frac{960\text{ W}}{1200\text{ W}} \times 100\% = 0.80 \times 100\% = 80\%$$
5. Final Result
$\eta = 80\%$
• Engineering Note: The remaining $P_{\text{loss}} = 1200 - 960 = 240\text{ W}$ ($20\%$) is dissipated as heat and friction losses.

Q6: Electric Heater Resistance & Energy Consumed
Q6. An electric heater operates at a voltage of $230\text{ V}$ and draws a current of $8.5\text{ A}$. Calculate the resistance of the heater element and the total energy consumed if it runs continuously for $2.5\text{ hours}$.
🔥 1. Given Data (SI & Practical Units)

• Voltage ($V$): $230\text{ V}$
• Current ($I$): $8.5\text{ A}$
• Time ($t$): $2.5\text{ hours} = 2.5 \times 3600\text{ s} = 9000\text{ s}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$R = \frac{V}{I} \qquad\text{and}\qquad E = P \cdot t = (V \cdot I) \cdot t$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Heater Element Resistance ($R$):
$$R = \frac{230\text{ V}}{8.5\text{ A}} = 27.0588\ \Omega \approx 27.06\ \Omega$$
2. Operating Power ($P$):
$$P = 230\text{ V} \times 8.5\text{ A} = 1955\text{ W} = 1.955\text{ kW}$$
3. Total Electrical Energy Consumed ($E$):
• In Kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$): $$E = 1.955\text{ kW} \times 2.5\text{ h} = 4.8875\text{ kWh}$$
• In Joules ($\text{J}$): $$E = 1955\text{ W} \times 9000\text{ s} = 17,595,000\text{ J} = 17.595\text{ MJ}$$
5. Final Result
$R = 27.06\ \Omega$  |  $E = 4.8875\text{ kWh}\ (17.595\text{ MJ})$
Q7: 100-Meter Resistor Wire Current & Resistance
Q7. A $100\text{-meter}$ resistor wire dissipates $60\text{ W}$ when connected across a $12\text{ V}$ DC source. Determine the current flowing through the wire and its resistance.
⚡ 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Wire Length ($L$): $100\text{ m}$
• Dissipated Power ($P$): $60\text{ W}$
• DC Source Voltage ($V$): $12\text{ V}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$I = \frac{P}{V} \qquad\text{and}\qquad R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{V^2}{P}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Current flowing through the wire ($I$):
$$I = \frac{60\text{ W}}{12\text{ V}} = 5\text{ A}$$
2. Total resistance of the wire ($R$):
$$R = \frac{12\text{ V}}{5\text{ A}} = 2.4\ \Omega \quad\left(\text{or } R = \frac{12^2}{60} = \frac{144}{60} = 2.4\ \Omega\right)$$
5. Final Result
$I = 5\text{ A}$  |  $R = 2.4\ \Omega$
• Engineering Note: The resistance per unit length of this wire is $2.4\ \Omega / 100\text{ m} = 0.024\ \Omega/\text{m}$.
Q8: Electrical Resistance of a Copper Wire
Q8. A copper wire has a length of $50\text{ m}$ and a cross-sectional diameter of $2\text{ mm}$. The resistivity of copper is $1.7 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$, calculate the total electrical resistance of the wire.
📏 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Length ($L$): $50\text{ m}$
• Diameter ($d$): $2\text{ mm} = 2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \implies \text{Radius } r = 1\text{ mm} = 1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$
• Resistivity ($\rho$): $1.7 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$
2. Physical Geometry of Cylindrical Copper Conductor
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$A = \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} \qquad\text{and}\qquad R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Cross-sectional area ($A$):
$$A = \pi \times (1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m})^2 = \pi \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2 \approx 3.1416 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$$
2. Total wire resistance ($R$):
$$R = \frac{1.7 \times 10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m} \times 50\text{ m}}{3.1416 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2} = \frac{8.5 \times 10^{-7}}{3.1416 \times 10^{-6}} \approx 0.27056\ \Omega$$
5. Final Result
$R = 0.2706\ \Omega\ (270.6\text{ m}\Omega)$
Q9: Stretching a Cylindrical Aluminum Wire
Q9. A cylindrical aluminum wire of length $L$ and radius $r$ has resistance $R$. If the wire is uniformly stretched to twice its original length without changing its total volume, determine its new resistance in terms of $R$.
🔄 1. Given Data

• Initial State: Length $L_1 = L$, Cross-sectional area $A_1 = \pi r^2$, Resistance $R_1 = R = \rho \frac{L}{A_1}$
• Final State (Stretched): New length $L_2 = 2L$, Constant Volume $\text{Vol}_1 = \text{Vol}_2$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$\text{Volume Invariance: } A_1 \cdot L_1 = A_2 \cdot L_2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Find the new cross-sectional area $A_2$ from constant volume:
$$A_1 \cdot L = A_2 \cdot (2L) \implies A_2 = \frac{A_1}{2}$$
2. Substitute $L_2 = 2L$ and $A_2 = \frac{A_1}{2}$ into the resistance formula:
$$R_{\text{new}} = \rho \frac{L_2}{A_2} = \rho \frac{2L}{A_1 / 2} = 4 \left(\rho \frac{L}{A_1}\right) = 4R$$
5. Final Result
$R_{\text{new}} = 4R$
• Engineering Rule: Stretching a wire uniformly by a factor $n$ multiplies its resistance by $n^2$ ($R_{\text{new}} = n^2 R$).
Q10: Ratio of Resistances of Two Conductors
Q10. Two conductors made of the same material have lengths in the ratio $3:1$ and radii in the ratio $1:2$. Find the ratio of their electrical resistances ($R_1 : R_2$).
📐 1. Given Data

• Same Material: $\rho_1 = \rho_2 = \rho$
• Length Ratio: $\frac{L_1}{L_2} = \frac{3}{1}$
• Radius Ratio: $\frac{r_1}{r_2} = \frac{1}{2}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$R = \rho \frac{L}{A} = \rho \frac{L}{\pi r^2} \implies \frac{R_1}{R_2} = \left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{L_1}{L_2}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^2$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
$$\frac{R_1}{R_2} = (1) \times \left(\frac{3}{1}\right) \times \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^2 = 3 \times 4 = \frac{12}{1}$$
5. Final Result
$R_1 : R_2 = 12 : 1$
Q11: Power Ratio of Two Parallel Copper Wires
Q11. Two cylindrical copper wires, A and B, are connected in parallel across a constant voltage supply. Wire A has a length $L$ and a cross-sectional radius $r$. Wire B has a length $2L$ and a cross-sectional radius $2r$. Deduce the ratio of the electric power dissipated in wire A to that in wire B ($P_A : P_B$).
🔀 1. Given Data

• Parallel Connection: $V_A = V_B = V$ (same voltage across both wires)
• Same Material (Copper): $\rho_A = \rho_B = \rho$
• Wire A Geometry: $L_A = L$, $r_A = r$
• Wire B Geometry: $L_B = 2L$, $r_B = 2r$
3. Governing Law / Formula
For parallel resistors across a constant voltage $V$:
$$P = \frac{V^2}{R} \implies \frac{P_A}{P_B} = \frac{R_B}{R_A} \qquad\text{where}\qquad R = \rho \frac{L}{\pi r^2}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Find the resistance ratio $\frac{R_A}{R_B}$:
$$R_A = \rho \frac{L}{\pi r^2}, \qquad R_B = \rho \frac{2L}{\pi (2r)^2} = \rho \frac{2L}{4\pi r^2} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\rho \frac{L}{\pi r^2}\right) = \frac{1}{2} R_A$$
$$\implies \frac{R_B}{R_A} = \frac{1}{2}$$
2. Substitute into the parallel power ratio:
$$\frac{P_A}{P_B} = \frac{V^2 / R_A}{V^2 / R_B} = \frac{R_B}{R_A} = \frac{1}{2}$$
5. Final Result
$P_A : P_B = 1 : 2$
Q12: Current & Power Dissipated in a 12 Ω Resistor
Q12. A resistive element of $12\ \Omega$ is connected across a $24\text{ V}$ battery with negligible internal resistance. Calculate:
a) The electric current supplied by the battery.
b) The power dissipated across the $12\ \Omega$ resistor.
🔋 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Resistance ($R$): $12\ \Omega$
• Battery Voltage ($V$): $24\text{ V}$ ($r_{\text{internal}} = 0\ \Omega$)
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$\text{a) } I = \frac{V}{R} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{b) } P = V \cdot I = I^2 \cdot R = \frac{V^2}{R}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
• Part (a) Electric current supplied by the battery:
$$I = \frac{24\text{ V}}{12\ \Omega} = 2\text{ A}$$
• Part (b) Power dissipated across the $12\ \Omega$ resistor:
$$P = I^2 \cdot R = (2\text{ A})^2 \times 12\ \Omega = 4 \times 12 = 48\text{ W}$$
5. Final Result
a) $I = 2\text{ A}$  |  b) $P = 48\text{ W}$
Q13: Monthly Electricity Cost of a Water Heater
Q13. An electric water heater is rated at $3\text{ kW}$, $220\text{ V}$. It is used daily for an average of $4\text{ hours}$. If the cost of electricity is $\$0.15\text{ per kWh}$, calculate the total cost of operating this water heater for a full month of $30\text{ days}$.
💰 1. Given Data

• Rated Power ($P$): $3\text{ kW}$
• Rated Voltage ($V$): $220\text{ V}$
• Daily Operating Time: $4\text{ hours/day}$ for $30\text{ days}$
• Electricity Tariff Rate: $\$0.15\text{ / kWh}$
3. Governing Law / Formula
$$E_{\text{total (kWh)}} = P_{\text{(kW)}} \times t_{\text{daily}} \times N_{\text{days}} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{Total Cost} = E_{\text{total (kWh)}} \times \text{Rate}$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
1. Total operating hours in 30 days:
$$t_{\text{total}} = 4\text{ hours/day} \times 30\text{ days} = 120\text{ hours}$$
2. Total electrical energy consumed in a month ($E_{\text{total}}$):
$$E_{\text{total}} = 3\text{ kW} \times 120\text{ h} = 360\text{ kWh}$$
3. Total monthly operating cost:
$$\text{Total Cost} = 360\text{ kWh} \times \$0.15/\text{kWh} = \$54.00$$
5. Final Result
$\text{Total Monthly Energy} = 360\text{ kWh}$  |  $\text{Total Cost} = \$54.00$

📌 Key Laws, Circuit Rules & Fault Diagnosis (Sheet 2A)
• Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL): $\sum_{\text{loop}} V = 0 \implies \sum V_{\text{rises}} = \sum V_{\text{drops}}$
• Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL): $\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$ at any node/junction
• Series Resistors: $R_T = R_1 + R_2 + \dots + R_n$, same current $I$ flows through all
• Parallel Resistors: $\frac{1}{R_T} = \sum \frac{1}{R_i}$, same voltage $V$ across all branches
• Point-to-Point Voltage: $V_{AB} = V_A - V_B$ (sum of potential drops from $A$ to $B$)
• Series Open Fault: $I = 0\text{ A}$, $V_{\text{intact}} = 0\text{ V}$, $V_{\text{open element}} = V_{\text{source}}$
• Series Short Fault: $R_{\text{shorted}} = 0\ \Omega$, $V_{\text{shorted}} = 0\text{ V}$, circuit current $I$ increases
• Parallel Open Fault: Open branch draws $I = 0\text{ A}$, reducing total current $I_T$ by $V_S/R_{\text{open}}$
Q1 — Part (1): KVL Analysis on Single-Loop Circuit — Fig. (1)
Q1 (1). Using Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL), analyze the circuit given in Fig. (1) to determine the unknown voltage $V_x$.
🔍 1. Given Data (From Fig. 1 Polarities & Values)

• Loop Current: $I = 2\text{ A}$ (flowing clockwise)
• Left Source ($9\text{ V}$): Top terminal is $(-)$, bottom terminal is $(+)$
• Element 1: $V_1 = 12\text{ V}$ with $(+)$ on left, $(-)$ on right
• Top Source ($24\text{ V}$): Left terminal is $(-)$, right terminal is $(+)$
• Element 2: Voltage $V_x$ with $(+)$ on left, $(-)$ on right
• Element 3: $V_3 = 16\text{ V}$ with $(+)$ on left, $(-)$ on right
• Right Source ($12\text{ V}$): Top terminal is $(-)$, bottom terminal is $(+)$
2. Circuit Diagram: Fig. (1) — Single-Loop KVL Analysis
3. Governing Law / Formula
By Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) around the closed loop in the clockwise (CW) direction (using the sign of the first terminal encountered for each element):
$$\sum_{\text{CW}} V = 0$$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
Starting from the bottom-left corner and moving clockwise (CW):
• Pass through $9\text{ V}$ source from $(+)$ to $(-)$: $+9\text{ V}$
• Pass through Element 1 from $(+)$ to $(-)$: $+12\text{ V}$
• Pass through $24\text{ V}$ source from $(-)$ to $(+)$: $-24\text{ V}$
• Pass through Element 2 from $(+)$ to $(-)$: $+V_x$
• Pass through Element 3 from $(+)$ to $(-)$: $+16\text{ V}$
• Pass through $12\text{ V}$ source from $(-)$ to $(+)$: $-12\text{ V}$
$$(+9) + (+12) + (-24) + V_x + (+16) + (-12) = 0$$
$$1 + V_x = 0 \implies V_x = -1\text{ V}$$
5. Final Result
$V_x = -1\text{ V}$
• Engineering Note: Since $I = 2\text{ A}$ enters the $(+)$ terminal of Element 2 and $V_x = -1\text{ V}$, Element 2 is actually supplying $P = |V_x \cdot I| = 2\text{ W}$ to the circuit.
Q1 — Part (2): Series Resistor Loop Analysis — Fig. (2)
Q1 (2). Using KVL, analyze the circuit given in Fig. (2) to determine:
a) The circuit current.
b) The total resistance of the circuit.
c) The value of the unknown resistance, $R$.
d) The voltage drop across all resistors in the circuit.
e) The power dissipated by all resistors.
⚡ 1. Given Data (SI Units)

• Source Voltage ($E$): $130\text{ V}$
• Known Resistors: $R_1 = 3\text{ k}\Omega = 3000\ \Omega$, $R_2 = 4\text{ k}\Omega = 4000\ \Omega$, $R_3 = 1\text{ k}\Omega = 1000\ \Omega$
• Power Dissipated in $R_3$ ($1\text{ k}\Omega$): $P_{1\text{k}\Omega} = 100\text{ mW} = 0.1\text{ W}$
• Unknown Resistor: $R$ (in series)
2. Circuit Diagram: Fig. (2) — Series Loop with Unknown Resistance R
3. Governing Law / Formula
• Current from Power: $P_{1\text{k}\Omega} = I^2 \cdot R_3 \implies I = \sqrt{\frac{P_{1\text{k}\Omega}}{R_3}}$
• Total Resistance & KVL: $R_T = \frac{E}{I} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + R$
• Voltage Drops & Power: $V_i = I \cdot R_i$, $P_i = I^2 \cdot R_i$
4. Step-by-Step Substitution
• a) Circuit Current ($I$): Since all elements are in series, the current is the same everywhere:
$$I = \sqrt{\frac{0.1\text{ W}}{1000\ \Omega}} = \sqrt{10^{-4}\text{ A}^2} = 0.01\text{ A} = 10\text{ mA}$$

$$I_4 = \frac{10\text{ V}}{560\ \Omega} = 17.86\text{ mA}, \qquad I_5 = \frac{10\text{ V}}{270\ \Omega} = 37.04\text{ mA}$$
2. Compare expected total current with the ammeter reading ($200.4\text{ mA}$):
$$I_{T,\text{expected}} = 45.45 + 100.00 + 10.00 + 17.86 + 37.04 = 210.35\text{ mA} \approx 210.4\text{ mA}$$
$$I_{\text{missing}} = 210.4\text{ mA} - 200.4\text{ mA} = 10.0\text{ mA}$$
3. Identify the faulty component:
The missing $10.0\text{ mA}$ matches the exact current of branch 3 ($I_3 = 10.0\text{ mA}$). Therefore, resistor $R_3\ (1.0\text{ k}\Omega)$ is open-circuited!
4. Actual Current and Power Dissipated in Each Branch:
• Branch 1 ($220\ \Omega$): $I_1 = 45.45\text{ mA}$,   $P_1 = 10\text{ V} \times 45.45\text{ mA} = 454.5\text{ mW}\ (0.4545\text{ W})$
• Branch 2 ($100\ \Omega$): $I_2 = 100.0\text{ mA}$,   $P_2 = 10\text{ V} \times 100.0\text{ mA} = 1000\text{ mW}\ (1.0\text{ W})$
• Branch 3 ($1.0\text{ k}\Omega$ — OPEN): $I_3 = 0\text{ mA}$,   $P_3 = 0\text{ W}$
• Branch 4 ($560\ \Omega$): $I_4 = 17.86\text{ mA}$,   $P_4 = 10\text{ V} \times 17.86\text{ mA} = 178.6\text{ mW}\ (0.1786\text{ W})$
• Branch 5 ($270\ \Omega$): $I_5 = 37.04\text{ mA}$,   $P_5 = 10\text{ V} \times 37.04\text{ mA} = 370.4\text{ mW}\ (0.3704\text{ W})$
(Sum of active branch currents: $45.45 + 100.0 + 0 + 17.86 + 37.04 = 200.35\text{ mA} \approx 200.4\text{ mA}$ ✓)
5. Final Result
Fault: Resistor $R_3\ (1.0\text{ k}\Omega)$ is open-circuited ($I_3 = 0\text{ A},\ P_3 = 0\text{ W}$) $I_1 = 45.45\text{ mA}\ (454.5\text{ mW}),\ I_2 = 100\text{ mA}\ (1.0\text{ W}),\ I_4 = 17.86\text{ mA}\ (178.6\text{ mW}),\ I_5 = 37.04\text{ mA}\ (370.4\text{ mW})$
