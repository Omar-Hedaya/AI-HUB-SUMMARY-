Part 1: Energy Transfer by Heat ($Q$)

Concept Governing Equation Physical Description & Key Parameters
Heat Transfer ($Q$) $$\text{Heat } (Q) \quad [\text{kJ}]$$ The form of energy transferred across a system boundary solely due to a temperature difference ($\Delta T$). Always flows spontaneously from the higher-temperature medium to the lower-temperature medium until thermal equilibrium is established.
Adiabatic Process $$Q = 0$$ A process during which the system exchanges no heat with its surroundings. Achieved either by perfect thermal insulation or when the system and surroundings are at the exact same temperature ($\Delta T = 0$).
Specific Heat ($q$) & Rate ($\dot{Q}$) $$q = \frac{Q}{m} \ [\text{kJ/kg}], \quad \dot{Q} = \frac{Q}{\Delta t} \ [\text{kW}]$$ $q$ is heat transferred per unit mass of the system. $\dot{Q}$ denotes the rate of heat transfer per unit time ($1\text{ kW} = 1\text{ kJ/s}$).
Heat Sign Convention $$Q_{\text{in}} > 0\ (+), \quad Q_{\text{out}} < 0\ (-)$$ Heat added to the system (Gain) is positive (+). Heat rejected or lost from the system to the surroundings is negative (-).

Part 2: Energy Transfer by Work ($W$)

Lecture 4 • Slide 6
👈 Drag horizontally on mobile devices to view full technical columns 👉
Concept Governing Equation Physical Description & Key Parameters
Mechanical Work ($W$) $$W = F \times \text{distance} \quad [\text{kJ}]$$ Energy transfer associated with a macroscopic force acting through a finite distance across the boundary (e.g., expanding piston, rotating shaft, electrical current).
Specific Work ($w$) & Power ($\dot{W}$) $$w = \frac{W}{m} \ [\text{kJ/kg}], \quad \dot{W} = \frac{W}{\Delta t} \ [\text{kW}]$$ $w$ represents work done per unit mass. $\dot{W}$ is mechanical power: the rate at which work is performed per unit time ($1\text{ kW} = 1\text{ kJ/s}$).
Work Sign Convention $$W_{\text{out}} > 0\ (+), \quad W_{\text{in}} < 0\ (-)$$ Work done by the system (e.g., fluid expansion pushing a piston) is positive (+).
Work done on the system (e.g., fluid compression by external load) is negative (-).

Component Mathematical Formulation Thermodynamic Meaning & Graphical Representation
Differential Work ($dW$) $$dW = F \cdot dx = (P \cdot A) \cdot dx = P \cdot dV$$ Differential work done when a piston with cross-sectional area $A$ moves by a displacement $dx$ under absolute internal pressure $P$.
Total Boundary Work ($W_{1-2}$) $$W_{1-2} = \int_{1}^{2} P \, dV \quad [\text{kJ}]$$ Definite integral evaluated along the quasi-equilibrium process path from initial total volume $V_1$ to final total volume $V_2$.
Area on $P-V$ Diagram $$\text{Area under curve} = W_{1-2}$$ The area under the process curve on a $P-V$ diagram equals the magnitude of the work done during expansion ($dV > 0$) or compression ($dV < 0$).

Part 4: Polytropic & Isentropic Thermodynamic Processes

Lecture 4 • Slides 10–14
👈 Drag horizontally on mobile devices to view full technical columns 👉
Process Name State Property Relations ($P-V-T$) Boundary Work Expression ($W_{1-2}$)
Polytropic Process
$$PV^n = C$$		 $$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^n$$ $$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{n-1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}$$ $$\mathbf{W_{1-2} = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - n} = \frac{m R (T_2 - T_1)}{1 - n}}$$ Models actual expansion and compression processes with piston friction ($n \ne 1$).
Isentropic Process
$$PV^\gamma = C \ (n = \gamma)$$		 $$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^\gamma$$ $$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}$$ $$\mathbf{W_{1-2} = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - \gamma} = \frac{m R (T_2 - T_1)}{1 - \gamma}}$$ Special ideal case: reversible and adiabatic ($Q = 0$), where $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$.

Part 5: Isothermal ($T = C$) & Isobaric ($P = C$) Processes

Lecture 4 • Slides 15–21
👈 Drag horizontally on mobile devices to view full technical columns 👉
Process Name State Property Relations ($P-V-T$) Boundary Work Expression ($W_{1-2}$)
Isothermal Process
$$T = C \implies PV = C$$		 $$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies \frac{P_2}{P_1} = \frac{V_1}{V_2}$$ $$\mathbf{W_{1-2} = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = m R T \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right)}$$
Isobaric Process
$$P = C \ (n = 0)$$		 $$P_1 = P_2, \quad \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$$ $$\mathbf{W_{1-2} = P(V_2 - V_1) = m P(v_2 - v_1)}$$

Part 6: Isochoric ($V = C$) & Complete 5-Process Master Summary

Lecture 4 • Slides 24–26
👈 Drag horizontally on mobile devices to view full technical columns 👉
Process Type Index ($n$) $P-V-T$ Property Relation Boundary Work Formula ($W_{1-2}$)
Isobaric (Const $P$) $n = 0$ $\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{V_1}, \quad P_1 = P_2$ $W = P(V_2 - V_1)$
Isothermal (Const $T$) $n = 1$ $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2, \quad \frac{P_2}{P_1} = \frac{V_1}{V_2}$ $W = P_1 V_1 \ln(V_2 / V_1)$
Polytropic (Actual) $n$ $P_1 V_1^n = P_2 V_2^n, \quad \frac{T_2}{T_1} = (\frac{V_1}{V_2})^{n-1}$ $W = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - n}$
Isentropic (Rev. Adiabatic) $n = \gamma$ $P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma, \quad \frac{T_2}{T_1} = (\frac{P_2}{P_1})^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}$ $W = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - \gamma}$
Isochoric (Const $V$) $n = \infty$ $\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \frac{T_2}{T_1}, \quad V_1 = V_2$ $W = 0 \quad (\Delta V = 0)$

Example 1 (Slide 12): Polytropic Expansion ($PV^2 = C$)

Problem Statement: $1\text{ kg}$ of a fluid is contained in a cylinder at initial pressure $P_1 = 20\text{ bar} = 2000\text{ kPa}$ and volume $V_1 = 0.05\text{ m}^3$. The fluid expands behind a piston according to $PV^2 = \text{constant}$ until the volume doubles ($V_2 = 2 V_1 = 0.10\text{ m}^3$). Calculate the work done in the process and sketch it on a $P-V$ diagram.

State 1: P₁ = 2000 kPa | V₁ = 0.05 m³ State 2: P₂ = 500 kPa | V₂ = 0.10 m³
Step 1: Calculate Final Pressure P₂ $$P_1 V_1^2 = P_2 V_2^2 \implies P_2 = P_1 \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^2 = 2000 \times \left(\frac{0.05}{0.10}\right)^2 = 2000 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \mathbf{500\text{ kPa}} = 5\text{ bar}$$
Step 2: Boundary Work for Polytropic Process ($n = 2$) $$W_{1-2} = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - n} = \frac{(500 \times 0.10) - (2000 \times 0.05)}{1 - 2} = \frac{50 - 100}{-1} = \mathbf{+50\text{ kJ}}$$ W = +50 kJ (Positive: work done by system)
Example 2 (Slide 17): Isothermal Compression of Air ($T = \text{const}$)

Problem Statement: A piston-cylinder device initially contains $0.4\text{ m}^3$ of air at $100\text{ kPa}$ and $80^\circ\text{C}$. The air is compressed to $0.1\text{ m}^3$ isothermally ($T = \text{constant}$). Determine the work done during this process.

State 1: P₁ = 100 kPa | V₁ = 0.40 m³ State 2: P₂ = 400 kPa | V₂ = 0.10 m³
Isothermal Closed System Work Formula: $$W_{1-2} = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = (100\text{ kPa}) \times (0.4\text{ m}^3) \times \ln\left(\frac{0.1}{0.4}\right) = 40 \times (-1.3863) = \mathbf{-55.45\text{ kJ}} \ (\approx -55.14\text{ kJ})$$ W = -55.14 kJ (Negative: work input to system)
Example 3 (Slide 18): Water Vapor Compression ($PV = \text{const}$)

Problem Statement: Water initially at $P_1 = 50\text{ kPa}$, $T_1 = 100^\circ\text{C}$, and $V_1 = 3\text{ m}^3$ is slowly compressed according to $PV = \text{constant}$ until a final pressure of $P_2 = 1\text{ MPa} = 1000\text{ kPa}$ is reached. Determine the work for this process.

State 1: P₁ = 50 kPa | V₁ = 3.0 m³ State 2: P₂ = 1000 kPa | V₂ = 0.15 m³
Step 1: Calculate Final Volume V₂ $$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies V_2 = \frac{P_1 V_1}{P_2} = \frac{50 \times 3}{1000} = \mathbf{0.15\text{ m}^3}$$
Step 2: Calculate Boundary Work $$W_{1-2} = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = (50 \times 3) \ln\left(\frac{0.15}{3}\right) = 150 \times (-2.9957) = \mathbf{-449.36\text{ kJ}}$$ W = -449.36 kJ (Work done on system)
Example 4 (Slide 20): Constant Pressure Steam Expansion

Problem Statement: A frictionless piston-cylinder device contains $4.5\text{ kg}$ of steam at $400\text{ kPa}$ and $150^\circ\text{C}$. Heat is transferred until the temperature reaches $200^\circ\text{C}$. The piston floats freely under constant pressure. Determine the work done by the steam.

Isobaric Expansion: P = Constant = 400 kPa
Step 1: Superheated Steam Table Lookup (Table A-6 at 400 kPa): At $P = 400\text{ kPa}$, $T_{\text{sat}} = 143.63^\circ\text{C}$. Since $T_1 = 150^\circ\text{C} > T_{\text{sat}}$, state 1 is superheated vapor. $$v_1 = 0.47084\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}, \quad v_2 (200^\circ\text{C}) = 0.53422\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$$
Step 2: Boundary Work Calculation: $$W_b = m \cdot P (v_2 - v_1) = 4.5\text{ kg} \times 400\text{ kPa} \times (0.53422 - 0.47084)\text{ m}^3/\text{kg} = \mathbf{+114.15\text{ kJ}}$$ W = +114.15 kJ (Expansion)
Example 5 (Slide 21): Isobaric Gas Expansion (0.5 MPa)

Problem Statement: A gas expands in a piston-cylinder assembly from $V_1 = 0.15\text{ m}^3$ to $V_2 = 0.36\text{ m}^3$ while the pressure remains constant at $0.5\text{ MPa} = 500\text{ kPa}$. Calculate the work done in $\text{kJ}$.

$$W_{1-2} = P(V_2 - V_1) = 0.5 \times 10^3\text{ kPa} \times (0.36 - 0.15)\text{ m}^3 = 500 \times 0.21 = \mathbf{+105\text{ kJ}}$$ W = +105 kJ (Work done by system)
Example 6 (Slide 22-23): Cylinder with Paddle Wheel & Piston

Problem Statement: A fluid in a cylinder expands from $V_1 = 0.040\text{ m}^3$ to $V_2 = 0.045\text{ m}^3$ at constant pressure of $690\text{ kPa}$. Concurrently, a paddle wheel performs $4880\text{ J}$ of work on the system. Find (a) work done on the piston, and (b) net work done on or by the system.

(a) Work done by the system on the piston: $$W_{\text{piston}} = P(V_2 - V_1) = 690\text{ kPa} \times (0.045 - 0.040)\text{ m}^3 = \mathbf{+3.45\text{ kJ}}$$
(b) Net work done on or by the system: Paddle wheel work is done on the system (input), so it is negative: $W_{\text{paddle}} = -4.88\text{ kJ}$. $$W_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{piston}} + W_{\text{paddle}} = 3.45 + (-4.88) = \mathbf{-1.43\text{ kJ}}$$ Wnet = -1.43 kJ (Net work done on the system)
Example 7 (Slide 25): Rigid Tank Cooling ($V = \text{const}$)

Problem Statement: A rigid tank contains air at $500\text{ kPa}$ and $150^\circ\text{C}$. Due to heat transfer to surroundings, pressure and temperature drop to $400\text{ kPa}$ and $65^\circ\text{C}$. Determine the boundary work done during this process.

Since the tank is rigid, there is no boundary displacement ($dV = 0$): $$W_{1-2} = \int_{1}^{2} P \, dV = \mathbf{0\text{ kJ}}$$ W = 0 (No boundary work for constant-volume processes)
Example 8A (Slide 27-29): 3-Process Closed Thermodynamic Cycle

Cycle Problem 8A: $1\text{ kg}$ of fluid undergoes a 3-process cycle:
• 1 → 2: Polytropic expansion ($PV^2 = C$) from $20\text{ bar}, 0.05\text{ m}^3$ to double volume ($0.10\text{ m}^3$).
• 2 → 3: Isobaric cooling ($P = \text{const}$) back to initial volume ($0.05\text{ m}^3$).
• 3 → 1: Isochoric heating ($V = \text{const}$) back to initial pressure ($20\text{ bar}$). Calculate work for each process and the net cycle work.

1-2: Polytropic Expansion (+50 kJ) 2-3: Isobaric Cooling (-25 kJ) 3-1: Isochoric Heating (0 kJ)
1. Process 1-2 (Polytropic Expansion, $PV^2=C$): $$W_{1-2} = \frac{P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1}{1 - 2} = \frac{(500 \times 0.10) - (2000 \times 0.05)}{-1} = \mathbf{+50\text{ kJ}}$$
2. Process 2-3 (Isobaric Cooling, $P = \text{const}$): $$W_{2-3} = P_2(V_3 - V_2) = 500\text{ kPa} \times (0.05 - 0.10)\text{ m}^3 = \mathbf{-25\text{ kJ}}$$
3. Process 3-1 (Isochoric Heating, $V = \text{const}$): $$W_{3-1} = 0\text{ kJ} \quad (\Delta V = 0)$$
4. Net Cycle Work and Heat: $$W_{\text{net}} = W_{1-2} + W_{2-3} + W_{3-1} = 50 + (-25) + 0 = \mathbf{+25\text{ kJ}}$$ By the First Law for a closed cycle: $$\oint \delta Q = \oint \delta W \implies Q_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{+25\text{ kJ}}$$ Wnet = +25 kJ (Net work produced by cycle)
Example 8B (Slide 30-31): Adiabatic-Isochoric-Isothermal Cycle

Cycle Problem 8B (Fig. 4.24): $0.1\text{ m}^3$ of ideal gas at $300\text{ K}$ and $1\text{ bar}$ is compressed adiabatically to $8\text{ bar}$ (1-2), cooled at constant volume (2-3), and expanded isothermally (3-1) back to start. ($C_p = 14.3, C_v = 10.2\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$). Find $P_3$ and net work/heat.

1-2: Adiabatic Comp (-20.27 kJ) 2-3: Isochoric Cooling (0 kJ) 3-1: Isothermal Exp (+14.82 kJ)
Step 1: Calculate Gas Constants & P₃: $$\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = \frac{14.3}{10.2} = 1.402, \quad R = C_p - C_v = 4.1\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$$ From adiabatic compression (1-2): $$V_2 = V_1 \left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right)^{1/\gamma} = 0.1 \times \left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^{1/1.402} = \mathbf{0.0227\text{ m}^3}$$ From isothermal process (3-1) where $P_3 V_3 = P_1 V_1$ ($V_3 = V_2$): $$P_3 = \frac{P_1 V_1}{V_3} = \frac{1 \times 0.1}{0.0227} = \mathbf{4.4\text{ bar}}$$
Step 2: Net Cycle Work Calculation: $$W_{1-2} = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{(1 \times 10^5 \times 0.1) - (8 \times 10^5 \times 0.0227)}{1.402 - 1} = -20.27\text{ kJ}$$ $$W_{2-3} = 0\text{ kJ} \quad (\text{Isochoric})$$ $$W_{3-1} = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{P_3}{P_1}\right) = (10^5 \times 0.1) \ln(4.4) = +14.82\text{ kJ}$$ $$W_{\text{net}} = -20.27 + 0 + 14.82 = \mathbf{-5.45\text{ kJ}}$$ $$Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{-5.45\text{ kJ}} \quad (\text{Heat rejected from cycle})$$ Wnet = -5.45 kJ (Net work input)
Example 8C (Slide 32-33): Isothermal-Isochoric-Polytropic Cycle

Cycle Problem 8C (Fig. 4.25): $0.15\text{ m}^3$ of ideal gas at $15\text{ bar}$ and $550\text{ K}$ expands isothermally (1-2) to 4 times initial volume, cooled at constant volume (2-3) to $290\text{ K}$, and compressed polytropically ($PV^{1.46} = C$) back to state 1 (3-1). Calculate net work and heat transferred.

1-2: Isothermal Exp (+311.9 kJ) 2-3: Isochoric Cooling (0 kJ) 3-1: Polytropic Comp (-230.87 kJ)
1. Process Work Calculations: Isothermal expansion (1-2): $$W_{1-2} = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = (15 \times 10^5 \times 0.15) \times \ln(4) = \mathbf{+311.9\text{ kJ}}$$ Constant volume cooling (2-3): $$P_3 = P_2 \left(\frac{T_3}{T_2}\right) = 3.75 \times \frac{290}{550} = 1.98\text{ bar}, \quad W_{2-3} = \mathbf{0\text{ kJ}}$$ Polytropic compression (3-1): $$W_{3-1} = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_3 V_3}{1 - n} = \frac{(15 \times 10^5 \times 0.15) - (1.98 \times 10^5 \times 0.60)}{1 - 1.46} = \mathbf{-230.87\text{ kJ}}$$
2. Net Cycle Work: $$W_{\text{net}} = 311.9 + 0 + (-230.87) = \mathbf{+81.03\text{ kJ}}$$ $$Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{+81.03\text{ kJ}}$$ Wnet = +81.03 kJ (Cycle output)

Assignment 2 (Slide 34): Piston Cylinder with Stops

Assignment Problem 2: A piston-cylinder device with stops at $40\%$ initial volume contains $0.6\text{ kg}$ steam at $1\text{ MPa}$ and $400^\circ\text{C}$. The steam is cooled. Determine the compression work if the final state is: (a) $1.0\text{ MPa}$ and $250^\circ\text{C}$. (b) $500\text{ kPa}$ (piston rests on stops). (c) Temperature at the final state in part (b).

1 to Stops: Isobaric Cooling (P = 1.0 MPa) On Stops: Isochoric Cooling (V = 0.4 V₁)
(a) Final state at $1.0\text{ MPa}$ and $250^\circ\text{C}$ (Piston floats): From Table A-6: $v_1 = 0.30661\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$, $v_2 = 0.23275\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. $$W_b = m P (v_1 - v_2) = (0.6 \times 1000) \times (0.30661 - 0.23275) = \mathbf{44.3\text{ kJ}}$$
(b) Piston reaches stops ($40\%$ initial volume): $$W_b = m P (v_1 - 0.4 v_1) = 0.6 \times 1000 \times 0.6(0.30661) = \mathbf{110.4\text{ kJ}}$$ Once resting on stops, further cooling to $500\text{ kPa}$ is constant-volume ($W = 0$).
(c) Final temperature at $0.5\text{ MPa}$: Final specific volume: $v_3 = 0.4 \times 0.30661 = 0.1226\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$.
At $0.5\text{ MPa}$, from Table A-5: $v_f = 0.001093$, $v_g = 0.37483\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. Since $v_f < v_3 < v_g \implies$ Saturated Liquid-Vapor Mixture at saturation temperature: $$\mathbf{T_3 = T_{\text{sat}} @ 0.5\text{ MPa} = 151.83^\circ\text{C}}$$
