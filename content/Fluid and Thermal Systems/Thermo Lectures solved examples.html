Example 1: Liquid Properties (Slide 20)
Calculate the specific weight ($w$), mass density ($\rho$), specific volume ($v$), and specific gravity ($S$) of a liquid having a volume of $V = 6\text{ m}^3$ and a total weight of $W = 44\text{ kN}$.
Step 1: Given Parameters
Volume: $V = 6\text{ m}^3$
Weight: $W = 44\text{ kN} = 44000\text{ N}$
Gravitational acceleration: $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$
Water reference density: $\rho_{water} = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$
Step 2: Specific Weight ($w$)
Formula: $$w = \frac{W}{V}$$
Calculation: $$w = \frac{44\text{ kN}}{6\text{ m}^3} = 7.333\text{ kN/m}^3 = 7333.33\text{ N/m}^3$$
Final Result: $w = 7.333\text{ kN/m}^3$
Step 3: Mass Density ($\rho$)
Formula: $$\rho = \frac{w}{g}$$
Calculation: $$\rho = \frac{7333.33\text{ N/m}^3}{9.81\text{ m/s}^2} = 747.54\text{ kg/m}^3$$
Final Result: $\rho = 747.5\text{ kg/m}^3$
Step 4: Specific Volume ($v$) & Specific Gravity ($S$)
$$v = \frac{1}{\rho} = \frac{1}{747.54\text{ kg/m}^3} = 0.001338\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$$
$$S = \frac{\rho}{\rho_{water}} = \frac{747.54}{1000} = 0.747$$
Final Results: $v = 0.00134\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$ and $SG = 0.747$
Example 2: Petrol Density & Weight (Slide 20)
Calculate the density ($\rho$), specific weight ($w$), and total weight ($W$) of one liter of petrol having a specific gravity of $S = 0.7$.
Step 1: Given Data & Unit Conversions
Volume: $$V = 1\text{ L} = \frac{1000}{10^6}\text{ m}^3 = 0.001\text{ m}^3$$
Specific gravity: $S = 0.7$
Step 2: Density ($\rho$) & Specific Weight ($w$)
$$\rho = S \cdot \rho_{water} = 0.7 \cdot 1000 = 700\text{ kg/m}^3$$
$$w = \rho \cdot g = 700 \cdot 9.81 = 6867\text{ N/m}^3$$
Results: $\rho = 700\text{ kg/m}^3$ and $w = 6867\text{ N/m}^3$
Step 3: Total Weight ($W$)
$$W = w \cdot V = 6867\text{ N/m}^3 \cdot 0.001\text{ m}^3 = 6.867\text{ N}$$
Final Result: $W = 6.867\text{ N}$
Example 1: Vertical Piston Force Balance (Slide 4)
A vertical cylinder-piston assembly contains gas. Piston mass plus load is $20\text{ kg}$. If $P_{atm} = 0.1\text{ MPa}$ and piston area is $10\text{ cm}^2$, calculate absolute and gauge gas pressures.
Step 1: Given Data
Mass: $m = 20\text{ kg}$, $P_{atm} = 0.1\text{ MPa} = 100000\text{ Pa}$
Area: $$A_p = 10\text{ cm}^2 = \frac{10}{10^4}\text{ m}^2 = 0.001\text{ m}^2$$
Step 2: Force Equilibrium & Absolute Pressure
$$P_{abs} \cdot A_p = P_{atm} \cdot A_p + m \cdot g$$
$$P_{abs} = P_{atm} + \frac{m \cdot g}{A_p} = 100000 + \frac{20 \cdot 9.81}{0.001} = 296200\text{ Pa}$$
Result: $P_{abs} = 296.2\text{ kPa} = 0.2962\text{ MPa}$
Step 3: Gauge Pressure
$$P_{gage} = \frac{m \cdot g}{A_p} = \frac{20 \cdot 9.81}{0.001} = 196200\text{ Pa} = 196.2\text{ kPa}$$
Result: $P_{gage} = 196.2\text{ kPa}$
Example 2: Piston Device with Spring (Slide 5)
Piston mass is $3.2\text{ kg}$, area is $35\text{ cm}^2$. A spring exerts a force of $150\text{ N}$ downwards. Atmospheric pressure is $95\text{ kPa}$. Find internal cylinder pressure.
Step 1: Force Equilibrium
$$P \cdot A = P_{atm} \cdot A + m_p \cdot g + F_{spring}$$
$$P = P_{atm} + \frac{m_p \cdot g + F_{spring}}{A}$$
Step 2: Substitution & Calculation
$$P = 95\text{ kPa} + \frac{(3.2 \cdot 9.81) + 150}{0.0035 \cdot 1000}\text{ kPa} = 95 + \frac{181.392}{3.5} = 146.83\text{ kPa}$$
Final Result: $P = 147\text{ kPa}$
Example 3: $CO_2$ Specific Gas Constant & Heats (Slide 17)
Relative molecular mass of $CO_2$ is $44\text{ kg/kmol}$. Specific heat ratio is $\gamma = 1.3$. Calculate $R$, $C_v$, and $C_p$.
Step 1: Specific Gas Constant ($R$)
$$R = \frac{\bar{R}}{M} = \frac{8.314}{44} = 0.18895\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
Result: $R = 0.189\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
Step 2: Specific Heats ($C_v$ and $C_p$)
$$C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{0.189}{1.3 - 1} = 0.63\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
$$C_p = \gamma \cdot C_v = 1.3 \cdot 0.63 = 0.819\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
Results: $C_v = 0.63\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ and $C_p = 0.819\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
Example 4: Air Heating in Rigid Vessel (Slide 18)
$10\text{ kg}$ air heated in rigid vessel from $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $100^\circ\text{C}$. Given $\gamma = 1.4$ and $R = 0.287\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$. Calculate $C_p, C_v, \Delta U, \Delta H$.
Step 1: Specific Heats
$$C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{0.287}{1.4 - 1} = 0.7175\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
$$C_p = C_v + R = 0.7175 + 0.287 = 1.0045\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
Step 2: Energy & Enthalpy Changes
$$\Delta U = m \cdot C_v \cdot (T_2 - T_1) = 10 \cdot 0.7175 \cdot 80 = 574\text{ kJ}$$
$$\Delta H = m \cdot C_p \cdot (T_2 - T_1) = 10 \cdot 1.0045 \cdot 80 = 803.6\text{ kJ}$$
Results: $\Delta U = 574\text{ kJ}$ and $\Delta H = 803.6\text{ kJ}$
Example 5: Tire Pressure & Temperature (Slide 11)
Tire gauge pressure rises from $210\text{ kPa}$ to $220\text{ kPa}$ at $P_{atm} = 95\text{ kPa}$. Initial temperature is $25^\circ\text{C}$. Find final air temperature.
Step 1: Absolute State Values
$$P_1 = 210 + 95 = 305\text{ kPa}, \quad P_2 = 220 + 95 = 315\text{ kPa}$$
$$T_1 = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15\text{ K}$$
Step 2: Final Temperature at Constant Volume
$$\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2} \implies T_2 = T_1 \cdot \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = 298.15 \cdot \left(\frac{315}{305}\right) = 307.9\text{ K}$$
Final Result: $T_2 = 34.8^\circ\text{C} \quad (307.8\text{ K})$
Example 6: Two Tanks Mixing to Equilibrium (Slide 11)
Tank A: $2\text{ kg}$ CO at $77^\circ\text{C}, 0.7\text{ bar}$. Tank B: $8\text{ kg}$ CO at $27^\circ\text{C}, 1.2\text{ bar}$. Mixing equilibrium is $42^\circ\text{C}$. Find final pressure $P_2$.
Step 1: Individual Tank Volumes
$$V_A = \frac{m_A \cdot R \cdot T_A}{P_A} = \frac{2 \cdot 0.29683 \cdot 350}{70} = 2.9683\text{ m}^3$$
$$V_B = \frac{m_B \cdot R \cdot T_B}{P_B} = \frac{8 \cdot 0.29683 \cdot 300}{120} = 5.9366\text{ m}^3$$
$$V_{total} = V_A + V_B = 8.9049\text{ m}^3$$
Step 2: Final Pressure
$$P_2 = \frac{m_{total} \cdot R \cdot T_2}{V_{total}} = \frac{10 \cdot 0.29683 \cdot 315}{8.9049} = 105.02\text{ kPa}$$
Final Result: $P_2 = 105\text{ kPa} = 1.05\text{ bar}$
Example 7: Engine Compression Ratio (Slide 12)
Engine compression ratio is $15 \text{ to } 1$. Initial state is $100\text{ kPa}$ and $30^\circ\text{C}$. Find final pressure when temperature reaches $980^\circ\text{C}$.
Step-by-Step Calculation
$$\frac{V_1}{V_2} = 15, \quad T_1 = 303\text{ K}, \quad T_2 = 1253\text{ K}$$
$$P_2 = P_1 \cdot \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) = 100 \cdot (15) \cdot \left(\frac{1253}{303}\right) = 6203\text{ kPa}$$
Final Result: $P_2 = 6203\text{ kPa} = 62.03\text{ bar}$

Example 9 / Ex 11: Perfect Gas Expansion (Slide 14)
Gas of mass $0.01\text{ kg}$ occupies $V_1 = 0.003\text{ m}^3$ at $7\text{ bar}, 131^\circ\text{C}$. Expands to $1\text{ bar}, 0.02\text{ m}^3$. Calculate $R$, $M$, and $T_2$.
Step 1: Gas Constant ($R$) and Molar Mass ($M$)
$$R = \frac{P_1 \cdot V_1}{m \cdot T_1} = \frac{700 \cdot 0.003}{0.01 \cdot 404.15} = 0.5196\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
$$M = \frac{\bar{R}}{R} = \frac{8.314}{0.5196} = 16.0\text{ kg/kmol}$$
Step 2: Final Temperature ($T_2$)
$$T_2 = \frac{P_2 \cdot V_2}{m \cdot R} = \frac{100 \cdot 0.02}{0.01 \cdot 0.5196} = 384.9\text{ K} = 111.8^\circ\text{C}$$
Final Result: $T_2 = 111.8^\circ\text{C}$
Example 10 / Ex 9: Process Enthalpy Decrease (Slide 19)
Gas at $P_1 = 510\text{ kPa}, V_1 = 142\text{ L}$ goes to $P_2 = 170\text{ kPa}, V_2 = 275\text{ L}$. Enthalpy decreases by $65\text{ kJ}$. $C_v = 0.718\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$. Find $\Delta U, C_p, R$.
Step 1: Internal Energy Change ($\Delta U$)
$$\Delta H = \Delta U + (P_2 V_2 - P_1 V_1)$$
$$-65 = \Delta U + (170 \cdot 0.275) - (510 \cdot 0.142) = \Delta U - 25.67$$
$$\Delta U = -65 + 25.67 = -39.33\text{ kJ}$$
Result: $\Delta U = -39.33\text{ kJ}$
Step 2: Specific Heats ($C_p$) and Gas Constant ($R$)
$$m \cdot \Delta T = \frac{\Delta U}{C_v} = \frac{-39.33}{0.718} = -54.777$$
$$C_p = \frac{\Delta H}{m \cdot \Delta T} = \frac{-65}{-54.777} = 1.187\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
$$R = C_p - C_v = 1.187 - 0.718 = 0.469\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$$
Results: $C_p = 1.187\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ and $R = 0.469\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
Example 11 / Ex 7: Real Gas $CO_2$ Tank & Error (Slide 22)
A $200\text{-L}$ tank contains $CO_2$ at $2\text{ MPa}$ and $35^\circ\text{C}$. Find actual real mass using compressibility factor $Z$, and find error percentage if ideal gas model is assumed.
Step 1: Reduced Properties & $Z$ Factor
$$P_r = \frac{P}{P_c} = \frac{2}{7.38} = 0.271, \quad T_r = \frac{T}{T_c} = \frac{308}{304.1} = 1.013$$
From Generalized Chart: $$Z = 0.90$$
Step 2: Mass Calculation & Percentage Error
$$m_{actual} = \frac{P \cdot V}{Z \cdot R \cdot T} = \frac{2000 \cdot 0.2}{0.90 \cdot 0.18892 \cdot 308} = 7.63\text{ kg}$$
$$m_{ideal} = \frac{P \cdot V}{R \cdot T} = \frac{400}{0.18892 \cdot 308} = 6.87\text{ kg}$$
$$\text{Error \%} = \frac{m_{actual} - m_{ideal}}{m_{actual}} \cdot 100 = \frac{7.63 - 6.87}{7.63} \cdot 100 \approx 10\%$$
Results: $m_{actual} = 7.63\text{ kg}$ and $\text{Error} = 10\%$

Example 1: Polytropic Expansion ($PV^2 = C$) (Slide 12)
$1\text{ kg}$ fluid expands behind a piston according to $PV^2 = C$ from $P_1 = 20\text{ bar}, V_1 = 0.05\text{ m}^3$ until volume doubles ($V_2 = 0.1\text{ m}^3$). Calculate work done.
Step 1: Final Pressure Calculation

$$W_{1-2} = \frac{P_2 \cdot V_2 - P_1 \cdot V_1}{1 - n} = \frac{(500 \cdot 0.10) - (2000 \cdot 0.05)}{1 - 2} = \frac{50 - 100}{-1} = +50\text{ kJ}$$
Final Result: $W = +50\text{ kJ}$

Assignment Problem 2: Vertical Cylinder with Stops (Slide 30)
Air in cylinder with stops: $A = 0.2\text{ m}^2, P_1 = 200\text{ kPa}, T_1 = 500^\circ\text{C} = 773.15\text{ K}$, height $h_1 = 2\text{ m}$. Stops at $h = 1\text{ m}$. Cooled to stops, then further cooled to $20^\circ\text{C} = 293.15\text{ K}$. Find $T$ at stops and final pressure.
Step 1: Temperature at Stops ($T_2$) (Constant Pressure)
$$\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2} \implies T_2 = T_1 \cdot \left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = 773.15 \cdot \left(\frac{0.2}{0.4}\right) = 386.58\text{ K}$$
Result: $T_2 = 113.4^\circ\text{C} \quad (386.6\text{ K})$
Step 2: Final Pressure ($P_3$) (Constant Volume)
$$\frac{P_2}{T_2} = \frac{P_3}{T_3} \implies P_3 = P_2 \cdot \left(\frac{T_3}{T_2}\right) = 200 \cdot \left(\frac{293.15}{386.58}\right) = 151.66\text{ kPa}$$
Result: $P_3 = 151.7\text{ kPa}$
