Sheet 1: Straight Line & Pair of Lines

Prepared strictly according to Prof. Dr. Mohamed Eid's syllabus: General Equations, Discriminant Analysis, Separation, and Angle Formulas.

Fundamental Laws of Lectures 1 & 2 (Dr. M.H. Eid)

  • • Slope Formula: \( m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \)  |  Point-Slope Equation: \( y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \)
  • • Equidistance Locus (Perpendicular Bisector): \( PA = PB \iff PA^2 = PB^2 \) \( (x - x_A)^2 + (y - y_A)^2 = (x - x_B)^2 + (y - y_B)^2 \)
  • • General Second-Degree Equation of Pair of Lines: \( ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 \) Condition of pair of lines: \( \Delta = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix} = 0 \)
  • • Angle Between Pair of Lines Formula: \( \tan \varphi = \pm \frac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} \)
  • • Nature of Lines: Real if \( h^2 > ab \), Coincident/Parallel if \( h^2 = ab \), Perpendicular if \( a + b = 0 \), Imaginary if \( h^2 < ab \).
(1) Find the Equation of the Straight Line
Problem 1 (i)
Question: Find the equation of the line passing through the points (2, -3) and (4, 3).
Step 1: Given Coordinates & Slope Calculation
Given: \( (x_1, y_1) = (2, -3) \), \( (x_2, y_2) = (4, 3) \).
Slope Law: \( m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{3 - (-3)}{4 - 2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3 \)
Step 2: Point-Slope Equation & Derivation
Formula: \( y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \implies y - (-3) = 3(x - 2) \implies y + 3 = 3x - 6 \)
Rearranging: \( 3x - y - 9 = 0 \implies \) 3x - y - 9 = 0
Problem 1 (ii)
Question: Find the equation of the line passing through the points (3, -2) and (2, 1).
Step 1: Given Coordinates & Slope Calculation
Given: \( (x_1, y_1) = (3, -2) \), \( (x_2, y_2) = (2, 1) \).
Slope: \( m = \frac{1 - (-2)}{2 - 3} = \frac{3}{-1} = -3 \)
Step 2: Point-Slope Equation & Derivation
Formula: \( y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \implies y - 1 = -3(x - 2) \implies y - 1 = -3x + 6 \)
Rearranging: \( 3x + y - 7 = 0 \implies \) 3x + y - 7 = 0
Problem 1 (iii)
Question: Find the equation of the line passing through the points (2, 2) and (-1, 2).
Step 1: Slope & Classification
Slope: \( m = \frac{2 - 2}{-1 - 2} = \frac{0}{-3} = 0 \). Line is horizontal (parallel to x-axis).
Step 2: Equation
Final Equation: y = 2  or  y - 2 = 0
Problem 1 (iv)
Question: Find the equation of the line passing through the points (0, 1) and (0, -2).
Step 1: Slope & Classification
Difference: \( \Delta x = 0 - 0 = 0 \implies m = \frac{-3}{0} \) (Undefined slope, vertical line).
Step 2: Equation
Final Equation: x = 0  (The y-axis itself)
Problem 1 (v)
Question: Find the equation of the line which is at equidistance from the points A(2, 1) and B(0, -4).
Step 1: Locus Formulation
Condition: \( PA^2 = PB^2 \implies (x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x - 0)^2 + (y + 4)^2 \)
Step 2: Algebraic Expansion
\( x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 + y^2 + 8y + 16 \)
\( -4x - 2y + 5 = 8y + 16 \implies -4x - 10y - 11 = 0 \implies \) 4x + 10y + 11 = 0
Problem 1 (vi)
Question: Find the equation of the line which is at equidistance from the points A(0, 1) and B(0, -1).
Step 1: Locus Formulation & Expansion
\( (x - 0)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x - 0)^2 + (y + 1)^2 \implies y^2 - 2y + 1 = y^2 + 2y + 1 \)
\( -2y = 2y \implies 4y = 0 \implies \) y = 0  (The x-axis)

Problem 2 (i)
Given: \( x^2 + 5xy - 6y^2 = 0 \)
Step 1: Separation into Two Individual Lines
Factorization: \( (x + 6y)(x - y) = 0 \)
Line 1: x + 6y = 0  (Slope \( m_1 = -1/6 \))
Line 2: x - y = 0  (Slope \( m_2 = 1 \))

\( a = 1, \; 2h = 5 \implies h = 5/2, \; b = -6 \implies a + b = -5, \; h^2 - ab = 25/4 + 6 = 49/4 \)
Formula: \( \tan \varphi = \pm \frac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} = \pm \frac{2(7/2)}{-5} = \pm \frac{7}{5} \)
Acute Angle: \(\varphi = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{7}{5}\right) \approx 54.46^\circ\)

Circle Theoretical Laws of Lecture 3 (Dr. M.H. Eid)

  • • Standard Equation: \( (x - x_1)^2 + (y - y_1)^2 = a^2 \quad | \quad \) General Form: \( x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 \)
  • • Center & Radius Formulas: Center \( C(-g, -f) \), Radius \( a = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c} \)
  • • Parametric Equations: \( x = x_1 + a\cos t, \quad y = y_1 + a\sin t, \quad (0 \le t \le 2\pi) \)
  • • Ends of Diameter Form: \( (x - x_1)(x - x_2) + (y - y_1)(y - y_2) = 0 \)
  • • Radical Axis Equation: \( S_1 - S_2 = 0 \iff 2(g_1 - g_2)x + 2(f_1 - f_2)y + (c_1 - c_2) = 0 \)
  • • Condition of Orthogonality: Two circles are orthogonal if and only if \( 2g_1 g_2 + 2f_1 f_2 = c_1 + c_2 \)

\( x = 2 + 3\cos t, \quad y = 3 + 3\sin t, \quad (0 \le t \le 2\pi) \)

(3) Radical Axis of Circles (S1 - S2 = 0)
Problem 3 (i)
Given Circles: \( S_1: x^2 + y^2 + 3x - 2y - 4 = 0 \)  and  \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - y - 6 = 0 \)
Step 1: Radical Axis Definition and Subtraction Law
Law: The radical axis of two circles is obtained by equating their power: \( S_1 - S_2 = 0 \)
Setting up the subtraction:
\( (x^2 + y^2 + 3x - 2y - 4) - (x^2 + y^2 - 2x - y - 6) = 0 \)
Step 2: Term-by-Term Subtraction
• Quadratic terms cancel: \( x^2 - x^2 = 0 \), \( y^2 - y^2 = 0 \)
• x-term: \( 3x - (-2x) = 3x + 2x = 5x \)
• y-term: \( -2y - (-y) = -2y + y = -y \)
• Constant: \( -4 - (-6) = -4 + 6 = +2 \)
Final Equation of Radical Axis: 5x - y + 2 = 0
Problem 3 (ii)
Given Circles: \( S_1: x^2 + y^2 - 3x + 4y - 31 = 0 \)  and  \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y + 3 = 0 \)
Step 1: Radical Axis Subtraction
\( S_1 - S_2 = 0 \implies (x^2 + y^2 - 3x + 4y - 31) - (x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y + 3) = 0 \)
Step 2: Term-by-Term Subtraction
• x-term: \( -3x - (-2x) = -x \)
• y-term: \( 4y - (-4y) = +8y \)
• Constant: \( -31 - 3 = -34 \)
Equation: \( -x + 8y - 34 = 0 \) → Multiplying by \(-1\): x - 8y + 34 = 0

Problem 3 (iv)

(4) Points of Intersection of Each Pair of Circles
Problem 4 (i)
Given Circles: \( S_1: x^2 + y^2 + 3x - 2y - 4 = 0 \)  and  \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - y - 6 = 0 \)
Step 1: Find the Line of Intersection (Radical Axis)
From Problem 3(i), the radical axis is:
\( S_1 - S_2 = 0 \implies 5x - y + 2 = 0 \implies y = 5x + 2 \)
Step 2: Substitute into Circle Equation
Substituting \( y = 5x + 2 \) into \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - y - 6 = 0 \):
\( x^2 + (5x + 2)^2 - 2x - (5x + 2) - 6 = 0 \)
Expanding terms:
\( x^2 + (25x^2 + 20x + 4) - 2x - 5x - 2 - 6 = 0 \)
Combining like terms:
\( (1 + 25)x^2 + (20 - 2 - 5)x + (4 - 2 - 6) = 0 \)
\( 26x^2 + 13x - 4 = 0 \)
Step 3: Solve the Quadratic for x and Find y
Using Quadratic Formula: \( x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \)
\( x = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{13^2 - 4(26)(-4)}}{2(26)} = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{169 + 416}}{52} = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{585}}{52} = \frac{-13 \pm 3\sqrt{65}}{52} \)
Since \( y = 5x + 2 \), the two points of intersection are:
\( P_1, P_2 = \left(\frac{-13 \pm 3\sqrt{65}}{52}, \; 5\left(\frac{-13 \pm 3\sqrt{65}}{52}\right) + 2\right) \)
Problem 4 (ii)
Given Circles: \( S_1: x^2 + y^2 - 3x + 4y - 31 = 0 \)  and  \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y + 3 = 0 \)
Step 1: Radical Axis Formulation
From Problem 3(ii): \( S_1 - S_2 = 0 \implies x - 8y + 34 = 0 \implies x = 8y - 34 \)
Step 2: Substitution & Quadratic Simplification
Substitute \( x = 8y - 34 \) into \( S_2: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y + 3 = 0 \):
\( (8y - 34)^2 + y^2 - 2(8y - 34) - 4y + 3 = 0 \)
\( (64y^2 - 544y + 1156) + y^2 - (16y - 68) - 4y + 3 = 0 \)
\( 65y^2 + (-544 - 16 - 4)y + (1156 + 68 + 3) = 0 \)
\( 65y^2 - 564y + 1227 = 0 \)
Step 3: Discriminant Analysis & Conclusion
Discriminant: \( \Delta = B^2 - 4AC \)
\( \Delta = (-564)^2 - 4(65)(1227) = 318096 - 319020 = -924 < 0 \)
Since the discriminant is strictly negative (\( \Delta < 0 \)), there are no real roots.
Result: No Real Points of Intersection (الدوائر متباعدة ولا تتقاطع في نقاط حقيقية)

Question: Determine the vertex, focus, directrix, axis of symmetry, and sketch the parabola: \( x^2 - 2x - 8y - 17 = 0 \)
Step 1: Completing the Square — Step-by-Step Derivation
(a) Separate Variables: Keep all terms in \( x \) on the LHS, move \( y \) and constants to the RHS:
\( x^2 - 2x = 8y + 17 \)
(b) Side Calculation (Completing the Square for x):
• Half of the coefficient of \( x \): \( \frac{-2}{2} = -1 \)
• Square of this value: \( (-1)^2 = 1 \)
• Add \( 1 \) to both sides of the equation:
\( (x^2 - 2x + 1) = 8y + 17 + 1 \)
(c) Factor LHS into a Perfect Square:
\( (x - 1)^2 = 8y + 18 \)
(d) Factor out the Coefficient of y (which is 8):
\( (x - 1)^2 = 8\left(y + \frac{18}{8}\right) \)
• Side Fraction Simplification: \( \frac{18}{8} = \frac{18 \div 2}{8 \div 2} = \frac{9}{4} \)
\( (x - 1)^2 = 8\left(y + \frac{9}{4}\right) \iff (x - 1)^2 = 8\left(y - \left(-\frac{9}{4}\right)\right) \)
Step 2: Comparison with Standard Form & Parameter Extraction
(a) Standard Form Formula: \( (x - x_1)^2 = 4a(y - y_1) \)
(b) Parameter Extraction:
• Vertex Coordinates: \( x_1 = 1, \quad y_1 = -\frac{9}{4} \implies \) \( V\left(1, -\frac{9}{4}\right) \)
• Focal Parameter \( 4a \): \( 4a = 8 \implies a = \frac{8}{4} = 2 \implies \) \( a = 2 \)
(c) Geometric Reasoning: Since \( x \) is squared, the axis is vertical (\( x = 1 \)). Because \( a = 2 > 0 \), the parabola opens Upwards (↑).
Vertex (V)
Rule: \( V(x_1, y_1) \)
Sub: \( V\left(1, -\frac{9}{4}\right) \) → \( V\left(1, -\frac{9}{4}\right) \)
Focus (F)
Rule: \( F(x_1, y_1 + a) \)
Sub: \( F\left(1, -\frac{9}{4} + 2\right) \) → \( F\left(1, -\frac{1}{4}\right) \)
Directrix (D)
Rule: \( y = y_1 - a \)
Sub: \( y = -\frac{9}{4} - 2 \) → \( y = -\frac{17}{4} \quad (4y + 17 = 0) \)
Axis of Symmetry
Rule: \( x = x_1 \)
Sub: \( x = 1 \) → x = 1

Final Form: x² + 2xy + y² - 14x + 2y + 17 = 0  •  Grouped: \( (x + y)^2 - 14x + 2y + 17 = 0 \)

Sheet 4 Theoretical Laws — The Ellipse (Dr. M.H. Eid Lecture 5)

  • • Horizontal Ellipse (\(a > b\)): \( \frac{(x-x_1)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-y_1)^2}{b^2} = 1 \)  |  Relation: \( a^2 = b^2 + c^2 \implies c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = ae \)
  • • Horizontal Elements: Vertices \( V(x_1 \pm a, y_1) \), Foci \( F(x_1 \pm c, y_1) \), Minor Ends \( M(x_1, y_1 \pm b) \), Major Axis \(= 2a\), Minor Axis \(= 2b\)
  • • Vertical Ellipse (\(b > a\)): \( \frac{(x-x_1)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-y_1)^2}{b^2} = 1 \)  |  Relation: \( b^2 = a^2 + c^2 \implies c = \sqrt{b^2 - a^2} \)
  • • Vertical Elements: Vertices \( V(x_1, y_1 \pm b) \), Foci \( F(x_1, y_1 \pm c) \), Minor Ends \( M(x_1 \pm a, y_1) \), Major Axis \(= 2b\), Minor Axis \(= 2a\)
  • • Parametric Form: \( x - x_1 = a\cos t, \quad y - y_1 = b\sin t, \quad 0 \le t \le 2\pi \)

Sheet 5 Theoretical Laws — The Hyperbola (Dr. M.H. Eid Lecture 6)

  • • Horizontal Hyperbola: \( \frac{(x-x_1)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-y_1)^2}{b^2} = 1 \)  |  Relation: \( c^2 = a^2 + b^2 \implies c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = ae \)
  • • Horizontal Elements: Vertices \( V(x_1 \pm a, y_1) \), Foci \( F(x_1 \pm c, y_1) \), Asymptotes \( y - y_1 = \pm \frac{b}{a}(x - x_1) \), Transverse \(= 2a\), Conjugate \(= 2b\)
  • • Vertical Hyperbola: \( \frac{(y-y_1)^2}{b^2} - \frac{(x-x_1)^2}{a^2} = 1 \)  |  Relation: \( c^2 = a^2 + b^2 \implies c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \)
  • • Vertical Elements: Vertices \( V(x_1, y_1 \pm b) \), Foci \( F(x_1, y_1 \pm c) \), Asymptotes \( y - y_1 = \pm \frac{b}{a}(x - x_1) \), Transverse \(= 2b\), Conjugate \(= 2a\)

General Second-Degree Equation Laws & Criteria (Dr. M.H. Eid Lecture 7)

  • • General Equation: \( ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 \)
  • • Discriminant Determinant: \( \Delta = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix} = a(bc - f^2) - h(hc - fg) + g(hf - bg) \)
  • • Case 1 — Degenerate Conic (\( \Delta = 0 \)):
    - If \( h^2 - ab > 0 \): Pair of intersecting real lines (\( a + b = 0 \implies \) perpendicular lines)
    - If \( h^2 - ab = 0 \): Pair of parallel or coincident lines
    - If \( h^2 - ab < 0 \): Point (pair of imaginary intersecting lines)
  • • Case 2 — Non-Degenerate Conic (\( \Delta \ne 0 \)):
    - If \( h^2 - ab < 0 \): Ellipse (Circle if \( a = b \) and \( h = 0 \))
    - If \( h^2 - ab = 0 \): Parabola
    - If \( h^2 - ab > 0 \): Hyperbola (Rectangular Hyperbola if \( a + b = 0 \))

Question (25)Intersection of Line and Sphere
Method: Substitute line parametric equations into sphere equation and evaluate the discriminant \( \Delta \).
(i) Line \( \frac{x-4}{-2} = \frac{y+2}{2} = \frac{z-3}{1} \), Sphere \( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 4 = 0 \)

\( (4 - 2t)^2 + (-2 + 2t)^2 + (3 + t)^2 = 4 \implies 9t^2 - 18t + 29 = 4 \implies 9t^2 - 18t + 25 = 0 \)
Discriminant: \( \Delta = (-18)^2 - 4(9)(25) = 324 - 900 = -576 < 0 \)
Conclusion: Line does NOT intersect the sphere (المستقيم لا يقطع الكرة)
(ii) Line \( \vec{r} = (4, 5, 8) + t(3, 0, -4) \), Sphere \( (x - 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2 + (z - 1)^2 - 25 = 0 \)
Parametric: \( x = 4 + 3t, y = 5, z = 8 - 4t \)
\( (3t + 1)^2 + 0^2 + (7 - 4t)^2 = 25 \implies 25t^2 - 50t + 50 = 25 \implies 25(t - 1)^2 = 0 \)
Exactly one repeated real root: \( t = 1 \implies \) Point of contact \( (7, 5, 4) \)
Conclusion: Line TOUCHES the sphere (المستقيم يمس الكرة)
(iii) Line \( \vec{r} = (4, 5, 8) + t(3, 0, -4) \), Sphere \( (x - 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2 + (z - 1)^2 - 50 = 0 \)
Parametric: \( x = 4 + 3t, y = 5, z = 8 - 4t \)
\( 25t^2 - 50t + 50 = 50 \implies 25t(t - 2) = 0 \implies t = 0 \) or \( t = 2 \)
Two distinct real roots \( \implies \) Points of intersection \( (4, 5, 8) \) and \( (10, 5, 0) \)
Conclusion: Line INTERSECTS the sphere in two points (المستقيم يقطع الكرة)

Classification of Quadratic Surfaces (Dr. Mohamed Eid — Lecture 8)

  • • Plane (المستوى): \( Ax + By + Cz + D = 0 \) (Linear equation of degree 1 in \( x, y, z \)).
  • • Sphere (الكرة): \( (x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 + (z - z_0)^2 = R^2 \).
  • • Elliptic Cone (المخروط الناقصي): \( \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} - \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 0 \) (Vertex at origin, axis along the negative-signed variable).
  • • Elliptic Paraboloid (المجسم المكافئ الناقصي): \( \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} = \pm 2py \) (Axis along the linear variable).
  • • Cylinder (الأسطوانة): Quadratic equation with one missing variable represents a cylinder whose generators are parallel to the missing axis (e.g. \( x^2 + z^2 = R^2 \) parallel to the y-axis).

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 2)

  • For a homogeneous pair, $h^2 > ab$ yields two real directions, $h^2 = ab$ gives a single repeated line, and $h^2 < ab$ yields two imaginary lines intersecting at $(0,0)$.
  • When $a + b = 0$, the lines are mutually perpendicular; in this case, the denominator in $\tan\phi$ becomes zero ($\phi = 90^\circ$).
  • Separating a general pair & Point of Intersection: Factor the quadratic terms into $(Ax+By)(Cx+Dy)$, attach constants $(Ax+By+m)(Cx+Dy+n)=0$, expand and match coefficients with $(2gx+2fy+c)$ to find $m, n$. The intersection point $(x_0, y_0)$ is obtained directly by solving $Ax+By+m=0$ and $Cx+Dy+n=0$ simultaneously.
  • Bisectors by Axis Translation: You can translate axes to intersection point $(x_0, y_0)$ using $x = X + x_0, y = Y + y_0$, apply $\frac{X^2-Y^2}{a-b} = \frac{XY}{h}$, then substitute back $X = x - x_0, Y = y - y_0$.
  • The angle between the lines depends strictly on the quadratic terms $(a, h, b)$; the linear and constant terms $(g, f, c)$ only control the shift and position of intersection.

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 3)

  • A real circle exists only when $r^2 = g^2 + f^2 - c > 0$. At $r=0$ it represents a single real point, while $r^2 < 0$ contains no real locus.
  • Length of tangent from $(x_1, y_1)$ is $L = \sqrt{S_1}$; the power of the point $S_1$ is the square of the tangent length.
  • Pair of Tangents from Point $(x_1, y_1)$ Through Slope $m$: Write line as $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. Equate perpendicular distance from center to radius $r$: $\frac{|A x_c + B y_c + C|}{\sqrt{1+m^2}} = r$, solve quadratic in $m$ for two slopes $m_1, m_2$, and multiply their equations.
  • Intersection of two circles: Subtract equations to find Radical Axis (linear equation), express $x$ in terms of $y$ (or vice versa), and substitute into one circle to find the two intersection points.
  • The Radical Axis is the common chord if two circles intersect, the common tangent if tangent, and is always perpendicular to the line connecting their centers.

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 4)

  • Sign of parameter $a$: Positive $a$ opens horizontal parabolas right and vertical parabolas upward; negative $a$ reverses opening direction.
  • The vertex is the midpoint between the focus and its perpendicular projection onto the directrix along the axis.
  • Shifted Parabola Tangent via Axis Translation: For $(y-k)^2 = 4a(x-h)$ at $(x_1, y_1)$, set $X = x - h, Y = y - k$ to obtain $Y^2 = 4aX$ with point $(X_1, Y_1)$. Apply $Y Y_1 = 2a(X + X_1)$, then substitute back $X$ and $Y$ (Dr. Eid's preferred method in Ex. 8).
  • Reflection Property (Theorem 2): The tangent at any point $P$ bisects the angle between the focal radius $PF$ and the perpendicular line from $P$ to the directrix.
  • Tangents at the endpoints of any focal chord are mutually perpendicular ($m_1 m_2 = -1$) and intersect directly on the directrix ($x = -a$).
  • Tangents at the ends of any set of parallel chords intersect on the corresponding diameter line ($y = 2a/m$).

Distance: Point to Plane $D = \frac{|a x_0 + b y_0 + c z_0 + d|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}$ Perpendicular distance from $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ to plane.
Distance: Parallel Planes $D = \frac{|d_1 - d_2|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}$ Planes with identical coefficients: $ax+by+cz+d_1=0$ and $ax+by+cz+d_2=0$.

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 9)

  • Parallel vs. Perpendicular planes: Parallel planes have proportional normal vectors ($\mathbf{N}_1 = k \mathbf{N}_2$); perpendicular planes have $\mathbf{N}_1 \cdot \mathbf{N}_2 = 0$.
  • Shortcut for Distance Between Parallel Planes: You don't need to match coefficients; simply choose an easy point on the first plane (e.g., set $y=z=0 \implies x = -d_1/a$) and calculate its normal distance to the second plane directly (Method in Lecture 9 Ex. 10).

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 10)

  • Three Methods for Skew Line Distance & Nearest Points:
    • Method I (Projection): $D = \frac{|\vec{P_1 P_2}\cdot(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{V})|}{|\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{V}|}$.
    • Method II (Nearest Points Parametric): Let $M = P_1 + t\mathbf{U}$ and $N = P_2 + r\mathbf{V}$. Set $\vec{MN}\cdot\mathbf{U}=0$ and $\vec{MN}\cdot\mathbf{V}=0$, solve for parameters $t, r$ to find exact coordinates of $M$ and $N$, then $D = |\vec{MN}|$.
    • Method III (Parallel Plane): Construct a plane through $P_1$ with normal $\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{U} \times \mathbf{V}$, then find the distance from $P_2$ on the second line to this plane.
  • For line-plane angles, always use the absolute value in the sine formula to report the acute geometric angle $0 \le \phi \le 90^\circ$.

$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2gx + 2fy + 2kz + c = 0$		 Center $C(-g, -f, -k)$, Radius $a = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 + k^2 - c}$.

Plane section of a sphere $r_{\text{section}} = \sqrt{a^2 - d^2}$ Cross-section is a circle of radius $r_{\text{section}}$ if plane distance from center $d < a$.

💡 Important Notes & Key Remarks (Lecture 11)

  • Nature of sphere: $a^2 > 0$ represents a real sphere; $a^2 = 0$ represents a single point; $a^2 < 0$ contains no real points.
  • Plane intersection geometry: A plane cuts a sphere into a real circle if $d < a$, touches at a single tangent point if $d = a$, and has no real intersection if $d > a$.
  • Sphere Tangent to Two Parallel Planes (Full Procedure): Pick point $P$ on plane 1. Write the normal line through $P$: $\frac{x-x_P}{A} = \frac{y-y_P}{B} = \frac{z-z_P}{C} = t$. Substitute into plane 2 to find intersection point $Q$. Center $C$ is midpoint of $PQ$, and radius $a = \frac{1}{2}|PQ|$.

Lecture 12: Quadric Surfaces

🪐 3D Quadric Surfaces: Ellipsoid, Cone, Paraboloid & Cylinder (السطوح التربيعية)
👈 اسحب الجدول أفقياً لتصفح كامل القوانين والوصف 👉
Surface / TopicStandard EquationIdentification & Features
Ellipsoid $\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-y_0)^2}{b^2} + \frac{(z-z_0)^2}{c^2} = 1$ Center $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$. Three squared terms with positive coefficients. Traces are ellipses.
Cone $\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-y_0)^2}{b^2} = \frac{(z-z_0)^2}{c^2}$ Vertex $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$. Axis along isolated variable ($z$). Plane slice through vertex gives pair of lines.
Elliptic Paraboloid $\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-y_0)^2}{b^2} = q(z - z_0)$ Vertex $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$. Two squared terms equal a linear variable. Axis along linear variable ($z$).
Hyperbolic Paraboloid $\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-y_0)^2}{b^2} = q(z - z_0)$ Saddle surface. Two squared terms of opposite signs equal a linear variable.
Hyperboloid (One Sheet) $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} - \frac{z^2}{c^2} = 1$ One negative squared term. Connected single-piece surface.
Hyperboloid (Two Sheets) $\frac{z^2}{c^2} - \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ Two negative squared terms. Disconnected two-sheet surface opening along $z$-axis.
Cylinder $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad y^2 = 4ax$ One coordinate missing completely. Surface extends infinitely parallel to the missing coordinate's axis.

💡 Key Recognition Strategy for Quadric Surfaces (Lecture 12)

  • Complete the squares in each variable ($x^2 + px = (x + p/2)^2 - p^2/4$) to reveal shifted centers and vertices.
  • Three positive squares equal to $1 \implies$ Ellipsoid.
  • Two positive squares equal to one square $\implies$ Cone (the isolated variable determines the cone's axis).
  • Two squared terms equal to a linear term $\implies$ Paraboloid (linear term dictates the opening axis).
  • Missing coordinate variable entirely $\implies$ Cylinder parallel to the missing coordinate axis.
  • Fixing a variable ($x=k, y=k, z=k$) yields 2D cross-sectional conic traces illustrating surface curvature.
